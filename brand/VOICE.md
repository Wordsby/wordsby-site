# Voice

## How we sound

Calm, plain, and literate. We write the way a thoughtful person talks across a table: short sentences, real words, no hype. Second person ("you") for the reader, "we" for Wordsby. The brand's own line sets the tone: *Build what you mean.*

We're talking mostly to business owners who need a website and don't want to learn website software. They aren't technical, and they shouldn't have to be.

## Say

- **"Use your words."** It's the core idea: you describe what you want in plain English, and that's how the site gets built and changed.
- **People and AI, together.** Every build has people on it, starting with a video call about your business. AI does the heavy lifting; people make sure it's right.
- **Fit.** Traditional builds take longer and often miss the mark. Wordsby gets to a site that fits your business.
- Be concrete about the process: a video call, a first draft, changes in plain English, launch.

## Don't

- **Don't name any AI provider or model.**
- **Don't mention pricing.**
- **Don't imply self-serve signup.** There's no "Sign up", "Get started free", or "Create your site". The action is joining the waitlist.
- Don't suggest there are no humans involved, or that it's "fully automated".
- Don't oversell: no "revolutionary", "seamless", "effortless", "unlock", "supercharge", "AI-powered", "magic".
- Don't invent numbers, speeds, or customer counts.
- For agencies only: Wordsby is Bring Your Own Key. Don't bring that up in copy written for business owners.

## Vocabulary

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| plain English, your own words | prompts, natural language processing |
| your site, your website | your web presence, digital experience |
| the waitlist | sign up, free trial, get started |
| people working with AI | AI-powered, automated |
| change, update, edit | iterate, deploy |

## Sample copy that sounds right

> You don't need to learn website software. Tell us about your business, and say what you want changed in your own words. We'll take it from there.
