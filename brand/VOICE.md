# Voice

_Example content. Replace with the client's real voice guidance during onboarding._

## How we sound

Warm, plain-spoken, and welcoming to beginners. We're a neighbor, not an institution. Short sentences. Second person ("you"), and "we" for ourselves.

## Do

- Lead with what the reader gets: "Try a free class," not "We offer introductory courses."
- Name the fear and dismiss it: "Never taken an art class? Most of our students hadn't either."
- Be concrete: "Six weeks, $180, all materials included."
- Mention sliding-scale pricing whenever cost comes up.

## Don't

- Don't use art-world jargon: "praxis," "interrogates," "multidisciplinary practice."
- Don't be precious about art. No "curated experiences."
- Don't oversell. No "world-class," "premier," "state-of-the-art."
- Don't use exclamation points more than once per page.

## Vocabulary

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| students, neighbors | clients, customers, patrons |
| classes | courses, workshops (unless it's a one-day workshop) |
| the studio | our facility |
| sliding-scale pricing | financial aid, scholarships |

## Sample copy that sounds right

> You don't need any experience. You don't need to bring anything. Just show up, and we'll hand you an apron.
