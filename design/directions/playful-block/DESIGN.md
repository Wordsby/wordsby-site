# Playful Block

Chunky, bright, and tactile. Rounded shapes, thick borders, and a short solid shadow under interactive elements so every button looks like it's waiting to be pressed. Friendly without being childish.

## Suits

Tutoring and learning centres, kids' activities and camps, family entertainment, pet care and grooming, driving schools, paediatric services, community programmes for families. Audiences who respond to encouragement and energy.

## Avoid for

Professional services, luxury, healthcare for adults, legal, finance, funeral services. The tactile cheerfulness undermines seriousness. Also avoid it when the client's palette is muted or dark — this direction needs a bright brand colour to work.

## Posture

- **Everything interactive looks pressable.** Buttons and cards sit on a short solid shadow and a thick border.
- **Rounded, generously.** Soft corners throughout, heavy rounded headlines.
- **Bright but disciplined.** The brand colour appears boldly — but still one accent, still on the actions that matter.
- **Encouraging copy.** Short, direct, warm. "Book a trial lesson" rather than "Enquire about our programmes".
- **Big tap targets.** Many visitors are on phones, some are children; make everything easy to hit.

## Do

- Use `feature-grid` with short, punchy items — this direction suits lists of activities, classes, or options.
- Keep headlines to a few words, set big and heavy.
- Use photos of real people mid-activity: smiling, doing, learning.
- Use the `muted` tone to separate sections rather than lines.

## Don't

- No emoji as icons, no clip art, no cartoon illustrations standing in for real photos.
- Don't stack exclamation marks. Energy comes from the shapes and colour, not punctuation.
- Don't use it for a client whose audience is adults making a serious decision.

## Fonts

Nunito at weight 800 for headings, Nunito Sans for body, when the client has none. Nunito's rounded terminals carry the friendliness so the layout doesn't have to try.
