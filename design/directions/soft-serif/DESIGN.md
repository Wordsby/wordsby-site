# Soft Serif

Calm, clinical, and quietly literate. A contemporary serif for headings, a clean sans for reading, soft rounded shapes, and restrained chrome that puts the words and the people first.

## Suits

Therapists and counsellors, private medical and wellness clinics, midwives and doulas, research organisations, foundations and grant-makers, independent schools, estate and elder-care planning. Clients who need to feel thoughtful, careful, and trustworthy without feeling cold.

## Avoid for

Anything loud, fast, or discount — retail promotions, trades, entertainment. Also avoid it for clients with very little written content; its restraint depends on words worth reading.

## Posture

- **A serif that thinks.** Headings in a contemporary text serif at a medium weight: considered, not ornate.
- **Restrained chrome.** Neutral canvas, soft rounded corners, the faintest shadow on imagery.
- **Content first.** Generous line length control and space around paragraphs; this direction is for reading.
- **Human photography.** Natural light, real people, calm settings. No stock smiles.
- **A calm accent**, used for the primary action only.

## Do

- Use `content` sections for genuine explanation — process, approach, what to expect at a first appointment.
- Answer the anxious questions early: cost, confidentiality, how to start.
- Use `faq` generously; this audience often has hesitations to resolve before they reach out.
- Keep the tone warm and plain. Short sentences, no clinical jargon.

## Don't

- Don't reach for the display serifs that mark generated pages (see `craft/anti-ai-slop.md`). The pairing here is deliberate.
- No beige-and-brass palettes by default. If the client's brand is calm, let their colours carry it.
- Don't add decorative leaves, waves, or blobs to signal "wellness".

## Fonts

Literata at weight 600 for headings, Figtree for body, when the client has none. Literata was designed for long reading on screens, so its headings feel literate rather than decorative.
