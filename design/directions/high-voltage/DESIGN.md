# High Voltage

A performance cockpit. Dark canvas, a single electric accent that slashes through it, extremely heavy wide display type, and dense, fast-reading layouts. It says "speed" before a word is read.

## Suits

Gyms and performance training, motorsport and auto performance shops, tattoo studios, esports and gaming venues, energetic nightlife, bike and board shops. Clients selling intensity and adrenaline.

## Avoid for

Anything calm, caring, or formal — healthcare, finance, law, childcare, weddings. Also avoid it when the client's brand colours are muted: this direction depends on one bright colour against black, and a dull accent makes it look like a broken dark mode.

## Posture

- **The heaviest possible headlines.** Display type at its boldest weight, wide and unapologetic. The weight is the personality.
- **One electric accent.** The client's brightest colour, and only on things that matter: the primary action, one key number, one highlight.
- **Dense and fast.** Compact rhythm, tight grids, short copy. More per screen than any other dark direction.
- **Contained by lines.** Charcoal hairline borders contain groups; no soft shadows.
- **Numbers are heroes.** Times, weights, horsepower, class sizes, prices — set them large.

## Do

- Put the strongest single number in the hero.
- Keep every section heading to two to four words.
- Use a `light` or `primary` band once per page to break the darkness.
- Check that the accent colour is readable on black — the tone system picks readable pairings, but a pale accent will still look weak.

## Don't

- No neon glow effects, gradients, or lens flares. The flat accent on black is enough.
- Don't use more than one accent colour.
- Don't write long paragraphs. If it needs explaining, this is the wrong direction.

## Fonts

Unbounded at weight 900 for headings, Inter for body, when the client has none. Unbounded is wide and heavy, so headlines feel like a machine; Inter keeps the dense body text legible on black.
