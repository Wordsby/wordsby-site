# Modern Utility

One sans family at several weights, tight tracking, firm structure. It reads as competent and current — the visual language of a company that does the job properly and doesn't need to decorate.

## Suits

Trades and contractors, B2B services, logistics, manufacturing, IT and technical services, medical and dental practices. Good when the client's proof is credentials, coverage, response time, or specifications rather than atmosphere.

## Avoid for

Anything selling warmth, craft, or leisure — restaurants, wellness, childcare, boutique retail. It will read as cold. Also avoid it when the client has no real photography, because its density needs something concrete to hold.

## Posture

- **One family, three weights.** Hierarchy comes from weight and size, not from mixing typefaces.
- **Tight display tracking.** Headlines are compressed and confident, never airy.
- **Information first.** Specifics beat adjectives: service areas, hours, certifications, response times, the actual list of what's included.
- **Structure is visible.** Soft corners, a hairline border, one subtle shadow level for cards. Nothing floats.
- **Denser than the other directions.** Standard rhythm, tighter gaps, more per screen — but never smaller type.

## Do

- Lead with what the client does and where they do it, in the first screen.
- Use `feature-grid` at three or four columns for services; keep item text parallel in length.
- Put credentials, licences, and guarantees where they'll be seen — they're the reason this audience calls.
- Prefer a real photograph of real work over any illustration.

## Don't

- No decorative flourishes, no oversized type for its own sake, no gradient anything.
- Don't centre body content. Left-aligned reads as businesslike.
- Don't let the accent spread. One primary button per screen; the rest is foreground and border.
- Don't use this direction and then write soft, atmospheric copy. The words and the shapes have to agree.

## Fonts

Geist at 400/600/700, used only when the client has no fonts of their own. One family across display and body is deliberate here: utility beats contrast.
