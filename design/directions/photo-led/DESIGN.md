# Photo Led

The photograph is the design. Type is deliberately restrained — the display ceiling is lower than every other direction — so nothing competes with the imagery. Generous space, quiet structure, one accent.

## Suits

Restaurants and cafés, hotels and venues, real estate, salons and spas, landscapers and builders showing finished work, makers and boutiques. Any client whose product is visibly appealing and who has, or will pay for, real photography.

## Avoid for

**Any client without good photographs.** This is the one hard rule: strip the images out and this direction has nothing left. Also avoid it for information-dense or technical clients, where restrained type means the actual content gets no hierarchy.

## Posture

- **Type stays out of the way.** Headings are modest by design. If a headline needs to be enormous to work, this is the wrong direction.
- **Images are large and few.** One strong photograph per section, never a mosaic of small ones.
- **Space does the separating.** Generous rhythm, no borders around content, no shadows.
- **One accent, on the primary action only.** Everything else is foreground and photography.
- **Copy is short.** A sentence beside a picture, not a paragraph under it.

## Do

- Lead with the best available image, full width or split with a short line of text.
- Insist on real photography during onboarding, and say plainly that this direction depends on it.
- Keep image aspect ratios consistent down the page — mixed crops read as careless here.
- Use plenty of white space around each image so it reads as chosen rather than placed.

## Don't

- No text over a busy part of an image. If there's no calm corner, put the words beside the picture.
- No stock photography. It's obvious, and in this direction it's the whole page.
- Don't compensate for weak images with bigger type. Fix the images or change direction.

## Fonts

Instrument Sans at two weights for both display and body, when the client has none. One quiet family is deliberate: the typography is scaffolding here, not the message.
