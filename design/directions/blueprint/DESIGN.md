# Blueprint

An engineering specification rendered as a website. A strict rectangular grid, zero radius, light-weight display type, hairline borders, and background layers instead of shadows. Authoritative because it's methodical.

## Suits

Engineering firms, manufacturers, laboratories, surveyors, environmental and technical consultancies, accountants, B2B industrial suppliers. Clients whose credibility rests on precision, standards, and process.

## Avoid for

Consumer brands selling warmth or delight, hospitality, anything for children. It will read as cold and bureaucratic. Also avoid it for clients who want to look bold or disruptive — this direction's authority is quiet.

## Posture

- **Rectangles are the system.** Zero radius on everything: buttons, images, inputs, cards.
- **Light display type.** Large headlines in a light weight read as confident and exact; heavy weights would read as shouting.
- **Layers, not shadows.** Separate sections by stepping the background (`light` → `muted`), and contain things with a single hairline.
- **A strict grid.** Columns align across sections. Items in a grid are the same shape and similar length.
- **Specifics over adjectives.** Standards, certifications, tolerances, capacities, locations.

## Do

- Use `feature-grid` at three or four columns for capabilities or services, with parallel item text.
- Put credentials and standards somewhere prominent; they're what this audience checks first.
- Keep one accent colour, used for actions and nothing else.
- Left-align everything except, at most, a closing call to action.

## Don't

- No rounded corners anywhere, no soft shadows, no gradients.
- Don't set headlines in a heavy weight to add impact. The light weight is the point.
- Don't write atmospheric copy. If a sentence has no fact in it, cut it.

## Fonts

Hanken Grotesk at weight 300 for headings and 400 for body, when the client has none. A neutral grotesque with a real light weight — the light display is what gives this direction its engineered calm.
