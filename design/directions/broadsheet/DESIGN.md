# Broadsheet

Magazine density. Condensed headline type, tight rhythm, square corners, one hot accent, and more on the page than the other directions allow. It reads as current affairs — a publication rather than a brochure.

## Suits

Clients with a lot to say and the content to back it: event venues, publications and newsletters, membership organizations, cultural programmes, schools with real schedules, anyone announcing things regularly. Good when there's a steady stream of news, dates, or listings.

## Avoid for

Single-service businesses with three paragraphs of copy — the density will expose how little there is. Also avoid it for luxury or calm positioning, and for older audiences, since compact rhythm plus condensed type reduces legibility.

## Posture

- **Condensed display, generous size.** Headlines are tall and narrow, so they can be large without eating the page.
- **Compact rhythm.** Sections sit closer than in any other direction. That's deliberate; don't loosen it.
- **One hot accent** on an otherwise neutral page: a kicker, a section rule, one button.
- **Square corners, hairline rules.** Structure comes from lines and alignment, not from containers.
- **The grid is visible.** Aligned columns, consistent gutters, few exceptions.

## Do

- Use `feature-grid` at three or four columns for listings, and keep item text tightly parallel.
- Date things. Dates and specifics are what make this direction feel alive.
- Let one image be large and full-width; keep the rest small and aligned.

## Don't

- Don't leave it half-full. This direction needs content; an airy page in Broadsheet looks unfinished rather than calm.
- Don't add a second accent, and don't tint the background. Neutral canvas, one hot color.
- Don't set body copy below 16px to fit more in. Compact means tighter spacing, never smaller type.

## Fonts

Anton for display, IBM Plex Sans for body, when the client has none. Anton is a single-weight poster condensed — use size and case for hierarchy, since it has no lighter weights.
