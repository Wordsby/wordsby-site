# Open Door

Big, bold, and optimistic. Massive heavy headlines, extra-round shapes, and a fresh, bright accent that makes something ordinary — money, insurance, paperwork — feel approachable instead of intimidating.

## Suits

Credit unions and community banks, independent insurance agents, tax preparers and bookkeepers for small businesses, benefits and legal aid, community health centres, housing and energy assistance programmes. Clients whose customers find the subject stressful and need to feel welcome.

## Avoid for

Luxury, high-end professional services, or anything that relies on exclusivity. Also avoid it for clients who need to look traditional and established — this direction is deliberately modern and informal.

## Posture

- **Massive, heavy headlines.** The boldest weight, set tight. Confidence is the reassurance.
- **Extra-round everything.** Buttons and cards take a generous radius; nothing has a sharp corner.
- **A fresh accent.** The brand's brightest colour on primary actions, with text chosen for contrast automatically.
- **Plain words about complicated things.** "Get help with your taxes" beats "Comprehensive tax preparation services".
- **Warm, generous rhythm.** Room to breathe, never crowded.

## Do

- State the benefit in the headline in five words or fewer.
- Answer "how much does it cost?" and "how do I start?" on the first screen or close to it.
- Use `faq` to take the fear out of the process.
- Use photos of real staff and real members of the community.

## Don't

- Don't use financial jargon or legal phrasing in headings.
- Don't set headlines in a light weight — the heaviness is what makes this direction friendly rather than corporate.
- No stock photos of handshakes, piggy banks, or calculators.

## Fonts

Outfit at weight 900 for headings and 400 for body, when the client has none. A geometric sans with a genuinely heavy weight; one family keeps the tone consistent from headline to fine print.
