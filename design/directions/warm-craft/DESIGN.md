# Warm Craft

Rounded, generous, and human. Softer shapes and a friendlier voice, without tipping into cute. It reads as a real place run by real people.

## Suits

Local services, food and drink, wellness and fitness, childcare and education, salons, family businesses, community organizations. Best when the client has photographs of actual people and actual premises.

## Avoid for

Anything that needs to read as authoritative or technical — legal, financial, medical specialists, B2B engineering. The rounded shapes undercut authority. Also avoid it when the only available imagery is stock, because this direction depends on faces and places to feel genuine.

## Posture

- **Rounded and tactile.** Generous corner radius, a visible two-pixel border, one soft shadow level.
- **People before product.** Photos of staff and customers do more here than any graphic.
- **Warm but specific.** Friendly copy that still answers the question: where, when, how much, what happens next.
- **Generous rhythm.** Sections breathe; nothing feels crammed.
- **Colour is allowed to show up more** than in the other directions — but still one accent, still at most twice per screen.

## Do

- Write the way the owner talks. Contractions are fine. Read it aloud before shipping.
- Use `testimonials` early — for this audience, other locals' opinions are the strongest proof.
- Make the practical details easy: hours, parking, whether to book, what to bring.
- Keep one photograph large rather than four small.

## Don't

- No cutesiness: no exclamation marks stacked up, no emoji, no playful nonsense words.
- Don't round *everything*. Buttons and cards, yes; the layout itself stays straight.
- Don't mistake warm for vague. "We'll call you back the same day" beats "we care about our customers".

## Fonts

Bricolage Grotesque for headings, Karla for body, used only when the client has no fonts of their own. Both have a slight informality without being novelty faces.
