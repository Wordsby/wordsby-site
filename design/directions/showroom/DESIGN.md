# Showroom

Very large, clean display type on bright neutral space, with the product or the work given almost all of the visual drama. The interface is engineered to disappear.

## Suits

Businesses selling something that looks good: architects, custom builders, kitchen and bath showrooms, furniture makers, cosmetic and dental practices, premium product retailers. Clients with high-quality photography of finished work.

## Avoid for

Clients without strong imagery — this direction's calm becomes emptiness without it. Also avoid it for services that need to explain a process in detail; the one-idea-per-screen rhythm fights long explanations.

## Posture

- **One idea per screen.** A headline, a sentence, an image. Then the next.
- **Tight, deliberate type at display sizes.** Headlines are large with firm negative tracking; body text stays calm and small by comparison.
- **Chrome is quiet.** Soft rounded corners, a whisper of shadow on imagery, nothing decorative.
- **The accent means action.** Reserve it for buttons and links that do something.
- **Alternate calm with density.** A spacious hero, then a tighter band of specifics, then space again.

## Do

- Lead every section with the image, not the text.
- Keep hero copy to a headline and one sentence.
- Use `muted` bands for specifications, materials, or options, then return to the page tone.
- Crop photography consistently down the page.

## Don't

- Don't fill space with icons or illustrations because a section feels empty. Use a better photograph or remove the section.
- Don't use more than one button style per section.
- Don't let body copy grow past two short paragraphs per section.

## Fonts

Plus Jakarta Sans throughout, bold for display and regular for body, when the client has none. One family keeps the interface out of the way of the imagery.
