# Black Tie

A dark, formal direction led by a high-contrast display serif. Slow, expensive, and quiet: generous space, very little on each screen, and type that looks engraved rather than printed.

## Suits

Fine dining, cocktail bars, jewellers, bridal and couture, boutique hotels, private members' venues, high-end salons. Clients whose price is part of the appeal and who have evening or interior photography.

## Avoid for

Anything that needs to read as affordable, fast, or practical — trades, clinics, family services. Also avoid it for text-heavy sites: a dark canvas and a thin-stroke serif make long reading tiring. If the brief says "welcoming" or "for everyone", this direction says the opposite.

## Posture

- **The serif is the event.** Display type is large, set at a medium weight so the hairline strokes stay elegant. Body text is a clean humanist sans that stays out of the way.
- **Restraint is the luxury.** One idea per section, lots of dark space around it. When unsure, remove.
- **Almost monochrome.** Foreground and background do nearly all the work; the accent appears once per screen, usually the reservation or enquiry button.
- **Photography is atmospheric.** Low light, texture, detail shots. Never bright stock.
- **No visible structure.** No cards, no borders, no shadows. Space separates everything.

## Do

- Let a single photograph fill a section with only a short line of type beside it.
- Keep paragraphs short. Two or three lines at most on a dark canvas.
- Use one `light` band per page at most, to give the eye somewhere to rest.
- Check small body text contrast carefully — thin, light text on black fails easily.

## Don't

- Don't set body copy in the serif. Its hairlines disappear at reading sizes on a dark background.
- No gold gradients, no ornamental flourishes, no script fonts. The typeface is already doing the formal work.
- Don't crowd the hero with offers, badges, or opening hours. Put the practical detail further down.

## Fonts

Bodoni Moda for headings at weight 500, Nunito Sans for body, when the client has none. A true Didone needs to be set large; below about 28px its contrast turns fragile, which is why headings use it and nothing else does.
