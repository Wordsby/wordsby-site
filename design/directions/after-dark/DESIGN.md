# After Dark

A dark page as the native medium, not a theme switch. Content emerges from near-black through hairline borders and careful contrast, with compressed display type doing the work. Reads as precise and current.

## Suits

Studios, agencies, photographers and videographers, music and nightlife, technical products, anyone whose work is visual and looks better on black. Excellent when the client has strong imagery — dark canvases make photographs glow.

## Avoid for

Anything with a lot of reading (long service explanations, policy-heavy pages, education), older audiences, healthcare, and any client whose palette is pale or pastel — light brand colors on black either vibrate or wash out. If the client's whole identity is warmth and daylight, this direction will fight it.

## Posture

- **Structure is drawn with hairlines**, never with cards or shadows. A 1px border at low opacity is the whole vocabulary.
- **Display type is compressed.** Tight negative tracking at large sizes is what makes it read as engineered rather than merely dark.
- **The accent is rationed hard.** On a dark canvas a bright accent shouts. One button, one eyebrow, nothing more.
- **Sections separate by tone, not by rule.** Use `muted` for a slight lift off the page; use `light` for one deliberate bright band if the page needs relief.
- **Imagery carries the page.** A dark layout with no photography is just a dark page.

## Do

- Let large areas stay empty. Darkness needs room or it reads as heavy.
- Use one bright band (`tone: light` or `tone: primary`) somewhere to break the page. All-dark for eight sections is monotonous.
- Check body text contrast carefully — the tone system handles pairings, but small grey-on-black text is the classic failure.

## Don't

- No glows, no neon, no gradient meshes. Those are the tells this direction is most likely to attract.
- Don't use it because it looks impressive in a screenshot. It costs readability, so it has to earn that.
- Don't mix in a light footer. The page is dark all the way down.

## Fonts

Inter Tight for display, Inter for body, when the client has none. The tighter face at display sizes is the point; regular Inter alone reads soft on black.
