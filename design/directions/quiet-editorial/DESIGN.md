# Quiet Editorial

A serif-led, print-like direction. Wide margins, hairline rules, no shadows. It reads as considered and institutional without being stiff — a well-set page rather than a marketing site.

## Suits

Nonprofits, arts and culture, law and professional services, consultancies, schools, anyone whose product is judgment or trust. Works well when the client has real photography and real writing.

## Avoid for

E-commerce, dashboards, anything with dense data or many competing calls to action. Also avoid it when the client's brand is loud — this direction whispers, and a bright palette fights it. If the brief says "modern" or "energetic", pick a different direction rather than fighting this one.

## Posture

- **Type carries the page.** Serif headings at generous sizes, sans body. Almost nothing else is doing work.
- **Structure is drawn with rules and space**, never with shadows or cards. Sharp corners, hairline borders.
- **Space is the luxury signal.** Sections breathe. When in doubt, add space rather than an element.
- **One accent, used twice at most per screen.** Usually the primary button and one eyebrow.
- **Imagery is framed, not full-bleed.** A single strong photograph beats a grid of small ones.
- **Restraint in section count.** One hero plus three to five body sections. If the page needs eight, the content needs editing.

## Do

- Set headings tight (the tracking tokens already do this) and let long headlines run to two lines.
- Use the `muted` tone for alternating sections; keep `dark` for the footer.
- Left-align most things. Centered text works for a hero or a closing call to action, nowhere else.
- Let a section be mostly empty if its one idea is strong.

## Don't

- No shadows, no rounded cards, no gradients. If a section needs separation, change its tone or add a rule.
- No more than two typefaces.
- No icon beside every heading.
- Don't reach for a serif that's become a signature of machine-made pages. If the brand has no fonts, the direction's pairing is deliberate — use it.

## Fonts

Newsreader for headings, Inter for body, used only when the client has no fonts of their own. Newsreader is a text-first serif with real weight range, chosen over the display serifs that appear on every generated page.
